# Manthan — Concept Note

> A workshop on structured thinking for AI-era product building.

---

## The shift

AI made building fast. It did not make building right.

---

## Before AI

For two decades, building a product experience meant walking a chain of steps. Each step paid off the one before. Each step set up the one after.

| Step | What it produced |
|---|---|
| Idea + scope | A problem worth solving, framed sharp |
| Research | Evidence about real users, in real situations |
| JTBD + archetypes | A picture of who the user is and the job they are hiring the product for |
| Pareto cut | A short list of features that earn their place |
| Direction brief | A felt sense of the product — tone, behavior, surface |
| Wireframes → prototype | A thing you could click |

It was slow. A small feature took weeks. The output reflected the user, because every step was anchored to the user. Skip a step and the next step exposed it.

---

## After AI, Round 1: vibe coding

Then AI arrived. The first wave was vibe coding.

> *"Build me a meditation app."*
> Eight seconds later: a meditation app.

The output looked polished. It felt fast. It also felt generic.

Nothing about that prompt knew the user. No JTBD. No archetype. No cut. The AI filled the gaps with averages. The result was a landing page for nobody in particular, and a flow that mimicked Calm without knowing why Calm works.

---

## After AI, Round 2: better prompts, same gap

The next wave got smarter. Longer prompts. More context. Screenshots, system instructions, taste references. The output got better-looking. The gap stayed.

| What got fast | What stayed broken |
|---|---|
| Generating UI | Knowing which UI is the right one |
| Producing screens | Choosing what *not* to ship |
| Writing copy | Writing copy this user would actually say |
| Mimicking quality | Producing quality grounded in a real user need |

The bottleneck was never the typing. It was the thinking.

---

## The trade we never had to make

A choice formed quietly in every team: ship fast and accept generic, or ship slow and keep the rigor. Both sides lose.

This trade is fake. AI is a velocity engine. Rigor is a thinking engine. They are not the same thing. You can have both — if you put the thinking in the right place.

---

## Manthan: the speed of AI, the rigor of pre-AI

Manthan puts the seven steps of pre-AI product building back into the loop. Each step still earns its keep. Each step still asks the spine question: *what does this user need to succeed at this job?*

What changes is the muscle. AI runs the parallel research, the synthesis, the screen generation. The human holds the structure — the gates between steps, the cuts, the final call on what the user actually needs.

The output is the artifact you used to spend six weeks producing, delivered in 80 minutes, without losing control of the quality.

---

## I repeat

Speed without structure produces decoration.
Structure without speed produces backlog.
Manthan is the merge.
