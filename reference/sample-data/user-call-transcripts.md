## Call 1 — Riya, 8 months as PM at a Series B fintech (Bangalore)

> "Honestly the worst part of my job right now is standup. Every morning my engineering lead asks 'what are the priorities this sprint' and I have like… a list. But I don't know if my list is *good*. I look at the team and they're all nodding but I can't tell if they're nodding because they agree or nodding because they've given up on me catching up.
>
> I read Lenny religiously. Every Tuesday and Friday. I've read like every classic PM book. But when I'm sitting in that standup at 9:30 AM, none of it helps. I just want someone to look at my prioritisation and say 'this is the thinking I'd do here.' Not generic advice. Specific to *my* sprint.
>
> I tried using ChatGPT for this. It gives me a textbook answer. It doesn't push back on me. It just agrees with whatever I say. Which is the opposite of what I need."

## Call 2 — Dev, 4 months as PM at an early-stage AI startup (Mumbai)

> "I came from engineering. Two years writing backend code, then I switched to PM because I wanted to do strategy. Three months in I'm like 'oh god what have I done.'
>
> The thing nobody tells you is that PM is mostly about asking the right questions. Engineering is about answering the right questions. Completely different muscle. And I haven't built it. I keep asking surface-level questions in user interviews. The user gives me what I asked for. I get back to my desk and realise I missed the actual insight.
>
> I'd kill for something that just gives me practice. Like — give me a fake user-interview scenario, let me ask questions, and tell me which questions I missed. Like a flight simulator. I don't need certification. I don't need a course. I need *reps*.
>
> I tried bootcamps. Too expensive, too time-consuming, too theoretical. I have a job already. I want to be better at the job *this week*."

## Call 3 — Shreya, 14 months as PM at a B2B SaaS company (Pune)

> "The way I learn right now is I make mistakes in real meetings and then beat myself up about them on the train home. I'll ask a stupid question in a customer call. Or I'll commit to a feature without checking with engineering first. And the next day I'll be like 'why did I do that.'
>
> What I really want is a way to make those mistakes in *low stakes* before I make them in *high stakes*. Right now my stakes are always high. Every conversation is a real conversation with a real user or a real engineer. I have no warm-up.
>
> Athletes have warm-ups. Musicians have scales. PMs have… meetings, I guess. There's no practice mode."

## Call 4 — Karan, 11 months as PM at a consumer app (Bangalore)

> "I've started bookmarking every product question I have during the week and asking ChatGPT on weekends. It's not great but it's something. The problem is ChatGPT doesn't *grade* me. I ask 'what would you do here', it gives me an answer, I either agree or I don't, and then we move on. There's no signal that *my* thinking is improving.
>
> If something could tell me — even imperfectly — 'that question you asked was a 6/10, here's what would have made it an 8' — that's enormously valuable. Right now my feedback loop is six months long. Six months until I see whether my decisions worked. Anything that shortens that loop, even artificially, is gold.
>
> Pricing? I'd pay ₹500 a month. Easily. I spend more than that on coffee."

## Call 5 — Aakash, 16 months as PM at a logistics startup (Delhi NCR)

> "Look, I've used Lenny, I've used Reforge for one cohort, I've read Marty Cagan twice. The content is great. The format is wrong for me.
>
> Lenny is too passive. I read it, I nod, I move on. Nothing changes in my behaviour. Reforge was the opposite — too intense, eight weeks of evenings, after which I had a certificate and felt no different at work.
>
> What's missing is something *daily* and *short*. 15 minutes. Like Duolingo for product thinking. Drop in, do a thing, get a score, move on. Habit-shaped, not course-shaped.
>
> Also — and this is important — I don't want it to feel like *learning*. Learning feels like homework. I want it to feel like *practice*. Like batting nets. You're not studying batting; you're batting."
